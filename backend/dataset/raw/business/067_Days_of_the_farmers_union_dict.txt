'Days of the farmers' union dictating policy are over' - Muir

'Days of the farmers' union dictating policy are over' - Muir There have been calls for the Agriculture Minister, Andrew Muir, to resign The Agriculture Minister Andrew Muir has said "the days of the Ulster Farmers' Union (UFU) dictating government policy are over". It comes after the UFU extended a vote of no confidence in the department to include the minister, the permanent secretary and the chief vet, over how a cull of 51 cattle was carried out in County Tyrone. The cows were shot in June, leading to complaints about the manner of the cull which was carried out within earshot of members of the public. The UFU President John McLenaghan had said there were concerns about the independence of a Department of Agriculture, Environment and Rural Affairs (Daera) review into the incident. Muir said he would continue to engage with the UFU and had spoken with its president on Friday morning. He added that the situation he had inherited in the department was "extremely challenging", with issues building up "due to wrong government policy previously". He said he was "arguably the first minister in the history of this state that is not beholden to one vested interest". Muir described some of the speeches at a protest outside Stormont on Monday as "pure electioneering" and called on his fellow politicians to "calm it down". Earlier this week, Muir said a death threat had been made against him . He also received one in June . On Friday, he said "the police will be coming looking" for those who threatened or abused anyone in public life. In a statement to BBC News NI, the Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI) said it does "not discuss the security of individuals and no inference should be drawn from this". "However, we will continue to do all we can to ensure that all of our politicians are supported and that they can undertake their role free from fear and intimidation," Insp Roberts said. The UFU also called for Daera's Permanent Secretary Katrina Godfrey and the Chief Veterinary Officer Brian Dooher to resign. In response, the senior civil servants' union the FDA said it was "entirely inappropriate" to call for the resignation of officials who were "duty-bound to serve ministers" and "could not defend themselves publicly". The minister has previously said there was no reasonable alternative to the cattle being euthanised on site and has insisted it was a last resort as officials had to act on a court order. An external review of the cattle cull was announced by Daera earlier this month , but the UFU's Executive Committee met on Thursday night. Ulster Farmers' Union president John McLenaghan said the UFU "no longer have confidence that the Department can deliver" In a statement on Friday the UFU said it "does not have the confidence in the independence of the review as presently constituted, including the scope of the Terms of Reference and appointment of a single person to conduct it. "The UFU has consistently maintained that an investigation into an incident of this seriousness must be demonstrably independent, robust and capable of commanding the confidence of farm families and the wider public." John McLenaghan said calling for resignations was not a decision his union had taken "lightly". However he added that confidence had been "severely damaged". He said the most recent event is "just one of many instances where farmers feel that the Department has lost touch with farmers". "We no longer have confidence that the department can deliver." A Daera spokesperson said they and the minister were aware of the decision. "An external independent review is already underway to review the action taken by Department officials in response to the Deprivation Order in Fivemiletown," they continued. "There are many strategic issues and challenges for agriculture. "It is vital we face up to those challenges together to restore our environment and ensure a sustainable future for agriculture".
