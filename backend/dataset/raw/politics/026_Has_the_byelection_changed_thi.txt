Has the by-election changed things for Clacton? Nigel Farage cast his vote at a new housing estate in Walton-on-the-Naze By-elections have a habit of thrusting a constituency into the limelight and for five or six weeks part of Essex has again found itself at the centre of a media storm. Clacton has been on every politician's lips with the seaside town's railway station the destination for day trips by activists, cabinet ministers and big Westminster names. There has always been a big issue behind every by-election - Europe, the behaviour of the previous MP, the performance of the government, or even murder. Despite that, local issues are also highlighted and debated and there can often be a by-election bounce for the local community, as public money suddenly gets spent on things. So will Nigel Farage's second victory change anything for the people of Clacton? The Mid Bedfordshire by-election in 2023, won by Labour, drew attention to the lack of infrastructure on new housing developments and the Corby by-election of 2012 put the future of Kettering Hospital into the spotlight. Was it any coincidence that after the Wellingborough by-election in 2024, which thrust the issue of "left-behind communities" into the spotlight, the town became one of the first places to receive Labour's "Pride in Place" funding? When Clacton's last by-election took place in 2014, it was triggered when the sitting MP Douglas Carswell quit the Conservatives to join Farage's old party UKIP. He made his resignation as MP not just about the Conservative government's policy on an EU referendum, but also access to local NHS services , which appeared to improve after he won the seat again. But in the Clacton by-election of 2026, there has been no local issue in the campaign. In fact, there has hardly been any discussion of local issues. The one organised hustings , external we know about, at Clacton-on-Sea's Princes Theatre, attracted 14 of the 34 candidates, and Farage did not attend. While there was talk about improving the town's High Street, cleaning up the beach and creating more jobs, there was no serious debate about how this would be done. Nigel Farage won more votes than last time (22,239 compared to 21,225 in 2024), but the turnout was lower (44% compared to 58%) and no major party stood against him This election was a referendum on whether Nigel Farage should stay on as Clacton's MP, even though he has not been found guilty of having done anything wrong. He was looking for the backing of his constituents and he now has it (albeit with no major party standing against him) which he hopes will silence his critics at Westminster and avoid another by-election if a parliamentary standards inquiry finds against him. However, there is still the possibility of another by-election being triggered if Farage is sanctioned by Parliament. But there is unlikely to be any change for Clacton. Its newly re-elected MP isn't promising to do anything new to help his constituents, and there is no issue which local politicians or officials will be urgently reassessing following this by-election. The constituency has been plastered in Reform placards and posters during the by-election campaign Named after the seaside town, the Clacton constituency also includes relatively affluent Frinton-on-Sea, England's most-deprived neighbourhood of Jaywick , and villages and farms inland. At the start of the campaign, Farage told us he had put Clacton on the map —promoting its tourism and supporting local business ventures.
